# The Problem - Fellow Consistency & Expense Tracker

1. **Consistency drops without external structure.** Daily coding practice, revision, and other habits fellows want to keep up (prayer/dhikr routines, exercise, reading) tend to fall apart once there's no set class time forcing them — and there's no lightweight way to see your own pattern before it's already been three weeks.
2. **Irregular income makes budgeting hard.** Many students receive money irregularly (allowance, hustle income, family support) and don't track it — money runs out before the next inflow because spending isn't categorized anywhere. A bank app shows transactions, not categories or a budget. A notebook gets abandoned after a few days.

**Persona**: a fellow trying to stay consistent with self-directed practice during the capstone, who also needs to know where their money is actually going before payday/allowance day.

**Why it matters**: this is the exact "engagement and consistency" the capstone is being graded on — a tool a fellow can actually use to see their own pattern, not just a class exercise.
  
  
  
  ## Problem Validation.

  We surveyed four students to understand their experiences with tracking daily habits and spending. 
  All four respondents had previously tried tracking, but they reported difficulties maintaining it consistently, with most stopping within four weeks. The main reasons for stopping were forgetting to update their tracker, losing interest because of repetition, and finding tracking time-consuming or complicated. 
  Respondents said they would like tracking to be faster and easier, with reminders, progress charts, goals, and useful statistics. All four respondents indicated that they would probably or definitely use an app that made tracking simpler and more consistent. 
  These responses suggest that consistency and ease of use are significant challenges that MIZAN TRACK can address.
